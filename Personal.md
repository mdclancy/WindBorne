I started to race sailboats through a summer program when I was 7 years old. I enjoyed it for no other reason than that it was a competition, and I was very competitive. In high school I chose it as my varsity sport because my dad encouraged it, and because the soccer team had Friday practices which was when the philosophy club met. These were bad reasons to make this decision, but I learned a lot through the experience about what I like. I liked competing, especially with a tool that I had to maintain and experiment with. I would play with the rig and stay tension before practices and races and adjust to learn how the boat behaved. In the off season I got a job working at the harbor where we practiced and learned to patch fiberglass hulls with epoxy and gelcoat. I also learned that I enjoyed and took pride in doing things that were a little miserable, like chipping ice out of the hull in mid-March or having to right a capsized vessel in 30 knot winds. When I graduated and moved up from 2-person dinghies to racing 8-crew keelboats with other amateurs, I learned that I liked working as part of a team of smart driven people. And as other parts of my life got more serious and stressful I came to appreciate having a hobby which took me away from shore and out of cell service for days at a time; where I could have the wind and spray on my face and hike out over the side of the boat as the rail dipped in the water.
